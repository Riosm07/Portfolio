Project 01 ● Real client

IEEE ICAD 2026 Conference

Marketing team · Northeastern University · Spring 2026
Audience researchContent calendarMultichannelA/B testing
The brief

A student marketing team was engaged to promote an international IEEE conference, with the work presented to the actual client. The goal was to reach two very different professional audiences and drive registration.

What I did
  • Built personas for Policy Makers and Risk Management professionals to guide messaging and channel choices.
  • Contributed to a multi-week content calendar and a campaign deck across email, social, and event promotion.
  • Delivered an A/B testing recommendation for subject lines and calls to action.
The outcome

We presented the work to the client. Because we defined the personas and calendar first, the campaign spoke with one voice instead of a set of unconnected posts.

The concept

I invented a small coffee brand with Puerto Rican roots, launching to a bilingual audience in the U.S. I wanted to show how I hold one voice across email and social in both languages, instead of translating one into the other.

How I'd run it
  • Set a warm, roots-forward voice that reads naturally in English and Spanish.
  • Built a launch-week beat: teaser → announcement → launch-day email → follow-up.
  • Wrote paired A/B subject lines to learn whether heritage or flavor pulls the higher open.

Epic fantasy · opening chapter

Ashes of Virelios

A conquered heir, a stolen name, and a prophecy soaked in blood

Smoke didn't climb. It poured — thick, inky, viscous — seeping from the shattered skeleton of the city like festering gangrene. Theron stood atop the temple steps, bare feet scorched by smoldering soot, blood trickling from a ragged gash above his brow. He did not weep. Fear clenched his gut like a crushing fist, but Virelios's gods spurned tears. They exalted defiance. And now their altars lay hushed.

They stole his name before they bound him in chains. A hooded Aurelian commander proclaimed him dead by imperial decree, the last scion of Virelios struck from history. He was barely more than a child. Yet in Virelios, boys learned to bleed early.

“You will lose everything, and still you will stand.”

Why it's here A whole world has to arrive in the first paragraph — the stakes, the loss, the voice. That's the same discipline as a launch headline: earn attention in one line, then keep it.
Fantasy novel · world opening

Draconing

A city of stone and fire-proof forests, told from a dragon's point of view

Draconing, the capital of our country. Its infinite plains are covered with yellow, red and orange leaf trees — robust vegetation capable of surviving scorching flames. Necessary for a lively territory where the slightest disturbance can cause a wildfire.

Music, screams and roars. Today is the first day of a new year: the day when children who turned eight before the year began come to Draconing to find their soul dragon, a dragon born with their other half. Not all hatchlings find their human today. For us dragons, we cannot become adults without our other half. Unable to grow, unable to fill the void inside them. A piece missing, and not being able to fly and find it yourself.

“I remember how it felt — the anticipation, and the fear of this day.”

Why it's here Written in a non-human first person, which forces every detail to do double duty: describe the world and reveal who's describing it. Voice work, essentially — the same muscle brand writing uses.
